# Creators and production — source document

The reference behind `src/data/creators.ts` (`CREATORS`, `WORK_FACTS`) and the `/tribute`
page. Researched 6–7 November 2026.

## Policy

- Concise, public, professional facts only: works, collaborations, publication and
  production history.
- Every fact in the data file has **at least two sources**. Where sources disagree on a day,
  the data gives the month only.
- **Never:** private or biographical details, speculation about Tsugumi Ohba’s identity,
  quotations, claims about the creators’ intentions, opinions or feelings, or any suggestion
  that the creators know of or endorse this site.
- Fandom, Viz Media and Anime News Network pages failed TLS verification from the research
  environment; they were checked through search-engine retrieval. Wikipedia (English and
  Japanese) was fetched directly.

## Tsugumi Ohba — 大場 つぐみ — story

| Fact (as worded in data)                                                                                                        | Sources                                                                |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Wrote Death Note, credited for its story, with Takeshi Obata as artist.                                                         | Viz series page; EN Wikipedia _Death Note_; JA Wikipedia _大場つぐみ_  |
| Supplied the story as storyboards — rough layouts of the pages — which Obata turned into the finished artwork.                  | Viz interview with Ohba (2012); EN Wikipedia _Death Note_ (production) |
| Death Note began as a one-shot pilot in Weekly Shōnen Jump in 2003, before its serialization from December 2003 to May 2006.    | JA Wikipedia _大場つぐみ_, _DEATH NOTE_; EN Wikipedia _Death Note_     |
| Returned to the story with two special one-shots, “C-Kira” (2008) and “a-Kira” (2020), both drawn by Obata.                     | EN Wikipedia _Death Note_; JA Wikipedia _DEATH NOTE_, _大場つぐみ_     |
| Reunited with Obata for Bakuman. (Weekly Shōnen Jump, 2008–2012, 20 volumes) and Platinum End (Jump SQ, 2015–2021, 14 volumes). | EN Wikipedia _Tsugumi Ohba_; JA Wikipedia _大場つぐみ_                 |
| Also wrote the one-shot Skip! Yamada-kun (Weekly Young Jump, 2014), drawn by Robico.                                            | EN Wikipedia _Tsugumi Ohba_; JA Wikipedia _大場つぐみ_                 |

## Verified, but deliberately not used

- Obata’s early credits under another name, and both creators’ birthplaces or other personal
  details — not needed for a tribute and too close to private life.
- Anything the creators have said about characters, the ending or their own feelings (for
  example Ohba’s or Obata’s views on Light). These are opinions; the spec forbids presenting
  them, and paraphrase would risk misquoting.
- That Ohba and Obata worked largely through their editor. Public, but it describes their
  private working relationship more than the work itself.

## Not verified — do not use

- **Any speculation about Tsugumi Ohba’s identity**, or links between Ohba and other
  creators. Do not repeat, hint at or “debunk” it on the site.
- Awards credited to Death Note (an Eagle Award, a Tezuka Osamu Cultural Prize nomination,
  an Angoulême official selection, a Japan Media Arts Festival selection) — each found in
  only one source.
- Obata’s character designs for the game Castlevania Judgment, the Aoi Bungaku anime and the
  2016 live-action film — single source.
- Masaru Kitao as the anime’s character designer — single source (a 2007 Viz press release).
- The pilot’s exact on-sale day (one listing gives 4 August 2003) — only the issue is verified.
- The serialization’s last day: 15 May 2006 (English Wikipedia) vs 29 May 2006 (Japanese
  Wikipedia). Use “May 2006”.
- Anime air days: 3 Oct 2006 – 26 Jun 2007 (Japanese listings and the Death Note Wiki) vs
  4 Oct 2006 – 27 Jun 2007 (English Wikipedia). Use “October 2006 – June 2007”.
- The year of Obata’s Shogakukan Manga Award (1999 vs 2000, depending on how the award year
  is counted). The data omits the year.
- English dub premiere dates and broadcasters, sales and circulation figures, page counts of
  the one-shots — single-source or unverified.
- Any claim that the pilot’s “Death Eraser” or other pilot elements are part of the series.
- Any quotation, message or signature attributed to either creator. None appear on the site.
